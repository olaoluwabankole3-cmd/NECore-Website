Pillar 01 / Verification engine

Confidence starts at the source.

Manage individual and business verification cases, coordinate consent and evidence, and route outcomes for institutional review. Live source checks require authorized providers.

Source verification — NIN and BVN checks are targeted capabilities; synthetic BVN connectivity testing is supported through a sandbox adapter.
Document processing — Document evidence references and synthetic capture/review workflows; live authenticity and OCR checks remain subject to provider integration.
Biometric assurance — Hosted KYC sandbox sessions and status handling; live liveness and face matching are not available.

Government identity sources and specialist biometric checks require external approvals and provider agreements. Availability must be confirmed before production use.

Pillar 02 / Intelligence layer

Risk doesn't stop after onboarding.

Risk signal ingestion, explainable sample risk tiers and escalation are implemented as part of the case workflow. Institutions retain judgment over consequential outcomes.

Continuous scoring — event-driven score updates and risk tiers using illustrative rules, not a validated fraud model.
Behavioral anomalies — supported signal types include velocity anomalies, document mismatch, device reuse and behavior change.
Cross-platform intelligence — future capability subject to lawful access, privacy review and model validation.
Pillar 03 / Compliance infrastructure

Make every decision explainable.

Audit events, structured cases and controlled reporting create a traceable path from a verification request to the rationale behind a decision.

Policy rules — structured decision and reason codes today; versioned regulatory policy mapping is planned.
Human review — exceptions, reason codes and independent approvals.
Evidence exports — case audit history and KYB completeness/risk reports; formal regulatory templates remain planned.

Phase 2 / Future capability

An identity the customer can carry.

Our next product horizon is portable, digitally signed credentials that users can hold and selectively share. The intended model allows institutions to verify signed claims without repeatedly collecting raw identity numbers.

  • ✓User-controlled disclosure and sharing duration.
  • ✓Standard public-key cryptography and signature verification.
  • ✓No public blockchain or smart contracts required.
