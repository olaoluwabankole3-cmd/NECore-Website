Security should be visible in the architecture.

NECore is designed around purpose-limited verification, clear accountability, minimal exposure of identity data, and independent review for consequential decisions.

Security by design

Trust requires more than a badge.

The following describes implemented controls and remaining assurance work. Source-code review does not establish deployment security, regulatory approval or independent certification.

Protection of sensitive data

Case contracts reject raw national identity numbers and biometric content. Production encryption, retention and secure evidence storage require separate deployment validation.

Discuss safeguards

Least-privilege access

Tenant-scoped access and role checks are implemented; workforce identity and database isolation controls require target-environment validation.

Tamper-evident audit history

Cryptographically linked audit events, reason codes and integrity checks are implemented. Independent immutable retention is a separate production requirement.

Responsible verification

Never mistake uncertainty for approval.

A temporary provider outage, inconclusive biometric outcome, or conflicting data should produce an explicit state—not a confident but ungrounded decision.

  • ✓Inconclusive and high-risk cases remain reviewable.
  • ✓Consequential decisions can require independent approval.
  • ✓Authorized reviewers can inspect case history, reason codes and evidence references.

Privacy & accountability

A clear model for sensitive identity information.

Purpose limitation

Collect and process only information necessary for a valid verification purpose, with a documented legal basis.

Data minimization

Prefer masked claims and provider outcomes over unnecessary retransmission of raw identity numbers.

Retention & rights

Design clear retention, deletion and data-subject request workflows subject to lawful recordkeeping duties.

NECore does not claim regulatory approval, certification, independent security attestation or live identity-source authorization. Nigerian regulatory obligations and sector-specific policies must be assessed before production use.
